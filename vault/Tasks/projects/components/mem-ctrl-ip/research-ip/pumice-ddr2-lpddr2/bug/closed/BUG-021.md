# BUG-021: the arbiter issues ACTs closer than tRRD -- proved, with a reproduction

**Priority:** P2 — a JEDEC violation on real silicon if reached. NOT raised
higher because the sim campaign behind [[ISSUE-019]] found the tightest real
ACT-to-ACT spacing to be 10 cycles against a tRRD of 2, so the workloads that
actually run are nowhere near it.
**Status:** closed 2026-10-03 (FIXED — see the CLOSED section below)
**Owner:** TBD
**Found by:** `formal/pumice/cmd_arbiter`, composing `pumice_cmd_arbiter` with
the real `global_timers`.
**Reproduce:** `make -C formal/pumice/cmd_arbiter issue019` (fails, by design)
**Related:** [[ISSUE-019]] (predicted this and closed on "not observed"),
[[ISSUE-018]] (the readiness staleness, a different and now-fixed defect)

## What happens

Two ACTs to DIFFERENT banks, one cycle apart, with `t_rrd = 3`:

| cycle | `evt_act_o` | bank | `trrd_ok_i` | note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 1 | 2 | 1 | first ACT; timers correctly permit it |
| 6 | 1 | 0 | **0** | second ACT fires while the timers say NOT OK |

`age_act` at cycle 6 is 1 against a required 3. The timers are not at fault:
by cycle 6 `global_timers` has seen the cycle-5 ACT and is correctly reporting
`trrd_ok_i = 0`. The arbiter issues anyway.

## Why

The arbiter checks `tfaw_ok_i` / `trrd_ok_i` at `w_act_gate_live`, which is used
exactly once -- in the STAGE-1b pre-pick `always_comb` that produces
`w_sel_*_act_f`. That feeds the registered pre-pick, which feeds the main pick,
which feeds the output register: **two registers between the check and the
fire**. The final-stage `w_out_safe` re-validates an ACT against
`bank_act_ready_i[RK0][r_bank]` only, which is PER-BANK, and tRRD/tFAW are
rank-global. So a second ACT decided while the window was open still fires after
it has closed.

**This contradicts the design's own stated intent.** Both ACT masks carried a
comment saying tFAW/tRRD "are re-checked live at the fire stage
(`w_act_gate_live`), which is authoritative" -- the fire-stage recheck is what
the design intends and is not what the logic does. (Those comments were
corrected on 2026-09-28; the behaviour was not.)

## Why it is a proof and not a guess

* The **real** `global_timers` is instantiated and wired to the arbiter's own
  `evt_*` outputs. Proving the arbiter against free `*_ok_i` inputs would prove
  nothing -- the property is about the LOOP, which is what runs on silicon.
* The CAM-side inputs are free, which is the strongest environment, EXCEPT that
  the age-order matrix is constrained to a strict order -- and that is not a
  convenience, it is a theorem: `formal/pumice/rd_cmd_cam` proves exactly that
  about the block which drives this input. **The violation survives that
  constraint**, so it does not rest on a CAM state the real CAMs cannot produce.
* Two ACTs to different banks being simultaneously `bank_act_ready` is the
  normal case, not a contrived one. It is precisely why tRRD and tFAW exist as
  rank-global limits on top of the per-bank timers.

## What is NOT claimed

That it is reachable with the real CAM + bank-timer traffic. [[ISSUE-019]]'s sim
campaign armed a rank-global tRRD/tFAW check (proved live: inflating the window
to 30 fires it) and found **no violation across 171 tests at 14 operating
points**, with the tightest observed ACT-to-ACT spacing being 10 cycles. So this
is reachable in the composition as specified and not observed in the workloads
that run. Both statements are measured; neither contradicts the other.

## Fixing it

`w_out_safe` is the natural home -- it already re-validates the per-bank gate at
the fire, and adding `tfaw_ok_i[RK0] && trrd_ok_i[RK0]` there is what the
comments already claim happens.

**Read ISSUE-002's warning first.** Gating these two on the classify MASK cost
30% of the command bus: "the 3-stage pick pipeline then drained completely and
cost 3 more cycles to refill once it reopened ... the stream ran 6 commands then
stalled 5 cycles -- 30.77% against a 49% command-bus ceiling." Gating at the
FIRE is not the same thing as gating the mask -- a squashed fire costs one
bubble, not a pipeline drain -- but the change wants the same bandwidth
measurement afterwards that ISSUE-002 did.

`make -C formal/pumice/cmd_arbiter issue019` is the regression: it fails now and
must pass after.

## Observed in SIMULATION, 2026-10-01 (scoria)

This item records "Not observed in sim (ISSUE-019: 171 tests, tightest real
spacing 10 cycles against tRRD 2)". That is no longer the state of the
evidence: the same mechanism now has a ~30-second deterministic simulation
reproduction in scoria, which inherited this arbiter.

**scoria-ddr3-lpddr3 BUG-001** carries it: two cross-bank ACTs 1 cycle apart
against a tRRD of 2, on 3 of 3 seeds, measured at the ARBITER's own output (not
just at the scheduler output, so FIFO compression is ruled out by measurement
rather than argument) with `unsafe_pushes=0` -- i.e. `w_out_safe` approved the
second ACT.

What made it reachable there and not here is the OPERATING POINT, which is the
part worth carrying back: scoria's design point puts tRRD spacing at 2, the
tightest nonzero window, and its composed traffic keeps two CAM slots busy
across 8 banks so cross-bank ACTs are offered back to back. pumice's suite
never got ACTs closer than 10 cycles, so the margin hid the defect rather than
the defect being absent.

If this is fixed here, that repro is the cheapest available gate for the fix.

## CLOSED 2026-10-03 — fixed in `pumice_cmd_arbiter`, hold-not-drop

**Fix.** `w_out_safe` re-validates an ACT against `tfaw_ok_i[RK0] &&
trrd_ok_i[RK0]` at the fire, closing the two-register gap between the
STAGE-1b re-check and the command leaving. When the windows have shut in
that gap the pick is now HELD in the output register (`w_out_hold`) and
fires the cycle they reopen, not dropped: dropping re-traverses the 3-4
stage pick pipeline and re-races the same closing window. Hold cycles
book to `stall_actlimit_o` (TASK-006 attribution). The global_timers ok
outputs are strict flops of the counters, so the new gate terms add no
combinational loop.

**Verification (measured, this session).**
* `formal/pumice/cmd_arbiter` issue019 / prove / cover all PASS with the
  ISSUE019 assertions armed in every task — the held-red `issue019`
  reproduction is folded into the default suite, so
  `make -C formal formal-pumice` guards the fix directly.
  Mutation-checked: reverting only the fix flips `prove` back to FAIL at
  step 7 (`a_trrd_spacing`, t_rrd=3); restoring flips it green.
* Full pumice gate (`run-all-gate`, clean builds, both geometries):
  fub 80 passed, macro 48 passed, top 193 of 194 passed. The one top
  failure is `perf_paging_sched_cross`'s pref_row_first floor — red at
  HEAD before this fix as well (38.4% vs 40). See ISSUE-020.

**Measured cost (the ISSUE-002 measurement, repeated).**
`perf_paging_sweep` static_close at board geometry: 21.12% at HEAD
pre-fix (already below the 26.95% floor) -> 30.14% with the fix, back at
the 30.77% accepted calibration. A drop-not-fire variant of the same
gate measured 18.57% and broke `perf_write_ceiling` (20 W-stall cycles
vs budget 16) — that is why hold-not-drop. At DEFAULT geometry the fix
breaks the sweep's exact-100% claim (100.00% -> 90.67%): with the window
actually enforced, the 8-bank close-page stream cannot keep every write
beat contiguous through tFAW waits while the single-issue output
register parks the held ACT in front of ready columns. That claim, like
the board-geometry floors, was calibrated against pre-compliant window
behavior: reverting ONLY global_timers to pre-ISSUE-018 measures 42.01%
where HEAD measures 21.12%, isolating the dominant cause to the
next-state readiness rework's deferred bandwidth cost. Re-pinning these
assertions (or funding a skid/bypass so holds stop head-of-line
blocking) is ISSUE-020 and needs Sean's call, exactly as ISSUE-002's
original acceptance did.
