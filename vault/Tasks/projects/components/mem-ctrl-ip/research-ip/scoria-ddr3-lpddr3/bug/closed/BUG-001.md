# BUG-001: the arbiter issues two cross-bank ACTs 1 cycle apart, violating tRRD

**Priority:** P1 — a JEDEC timing violation on the command bus. At the shipping
design point tRRD is 15 ns (max(6 nCK, 10 ns) at CK 2.5 ns) and the part is
being given 10 ns. A DRAM that ignores the second activate leaves the bank
closed, and the read that follows returns an undriven bus; one that half-takes
it corrupts the row. Either way it is silent in simulation without the checker.
**Status:** CLOSED 2026-10-01. Fixed in the same session that found it, and the
timing gate that held it open is now answered by measurement (see the end):
the fix costs 0.172 ns, and the 100 MHz miss it was being blamed on predates
it by 1.85 ns and is scoria BUG-003.
Still OPEN because one gate in "Done when" below cannot be run yet: there is
no scoria FPGA build to time the change on. Do not close it on the
simulation evidence alone.
**Found:** 2026-10-01, by the first COMPOSED scoria test. Seventeen FUB suites
had passed; none could see this, because each mocked the arbiter's neighbours.

## Reproduction

```
source $REPO_ROOT/env_python
cd projects/components/mem-ctrl-ip/research-ip/scoria-ddr3-lpddr3/dv/tests
SEED=734 make 'run-scoria_mem_cmd_scheduler-func' AREAS=macro
```

Dies in ~30 s with the bound history checker's own message:

```
CMD_HISTORY @1740000: GLOBAL tRRD violation -- rank0 bank2 ACT only 1 cyc
after another ACT (need 2) -- cross-bank activate rate limit
```

Reproduces on **3 of 3 seeds tried** (734, 1, 2), on different banks (2, 2, 5),
so it is structural rather than a seed artifact.

## It is the ARBITER, not the FIFO -- measured, not inferred

The checker is bound at the scheduler's OUTPUT (`cmd_valid_o && cmd_ready_i`),
which sits downstream of `CMD_DELAY` and the 16-deep command FIFO. A violation
there has two candidate causes needing different fixes:

  (a) the arbiter issued the two ACTs too close;
  (b) the arbiter spaced them and the delay/FIFO path COMPRESSED them -- the
      TASK-007 class, "preserves ORDER but not SPACING", which has already
      produced a board failure in this family (REF->ACT compressed 15 cycles
      to 3, inside tRFC).

`ScoriaMemCmdSchedulerTB._arbiter_audit` records the arbiter-side cycle of
every FIFO push, so both spacings come out of one run
(`probe_act_spacing`, SEED=734):

```
arbiter ACTs=42 tight=[(157, 158, 1), (216, 217, 1)]
output  ACTs=42 tight=[(164, 165, 1), (223, 224, 1)]
unsafe_pushes=0
```

The same two pairs appear on both sides, shifted by a constant 7 cycles. **The
FIFO compressed nothing** — (b) is ruled out. The arbiter issued them 1 cycle
apart.

`unsafe_pushes=0` sharpens it further: the arbiter's own final safety gate
(`w_out_safe`) APPROVED the second ACT. This is not a pick escaping a closed
gate; it is a gate that does not check this window at the point where it
matters.

## Mechanism

`w_act_gate_live = !w_rfc_busy && tfaw_ok_i[RK0] && trrd_ok_i[RK0]` re-applies
the rank-global windows, but `w_out_safe` — the last-moment re-validation
before a registered pick fires — covers only the PER-BANK gate. tRRD and tFAW
constrain ACTs to DIFFERENT banks, so no per-bank check can see them, and an
ACT already in the pick pipeline when the rank-global window closes still
fires.

That is exactly the mechanism **pumice BUG-021** describes, in the same words.
The relationship is worth stating precisely:

| | pumice BUG-021 | scoria BUG-001 |
|---|---|---|
| mechanism | identical | identical |
| evidence | formal only — "Not observed in sim (ISSUE-019: 171 tests, tightest real spacing 10 cycles against tRRD 2)" | **simulation, 3/3 seeds, ~30 s** |
| observation point | formal composition | the arbiter's own output, measured |

Why scoria reproduces what pumice's 171 tests could not: the operating point.
tRRD spacing is **2** here — the tightest nonzero window, one cycle of slack —
and the composed traffic keeps two CAM slots busy across 8 random banks, so
cross-bank ACTs are offered back to back constantly. pumice's suite never got
ACTs closer than 10 cycles, so its margin hid the defect.

## Ruled out

* **A programming error in the test.** `t_rrd_i` is driven with `prog['tRRD']`
  = 1, and the N+1 convention makes that a 2-cycle window — measured in
  `test_scoria_global_timers.py` (a window programmed to N is ready at index
  N). `HIST_T_RRD` takes `spacing['tRRD']` = 2, and the checker flags only
  distances 1..T-1, i.e. exactly a 1-cycle gap. The arbiter was told 2 and
  produced 1.
* **A miscalibrated checker.** `test_scoria_cmd_history_checker.py` proves the
  tRRD arm fires on a genuine 1-cycle cross-bank pair AND does not fire at
  exactly T (`legal_at_the_minimum`). Nine mutations, each required to kill the
  simulation.
* **Illegal stimulus.** The mock CAMs offer two schedulable entries in
  different banks. That is ordinary traffic; spacing them is the arbiter's job.

## Fix

Include the rank-global gates in the final re-validation for ACT picks, so
`w_out_safe` refuses an ACT whose tRRD/tFAW window has closed since the pick
was made — the same shape as the per-bank gate it already applies.

**Flag before changing it:** this adds terms to the arbiter's final decision
cone, and that cone is the one the RTL comments call out as timing-critical
("the pick cone that once missed 75 MHz by 21 ps"). The board WNS for this
family is deliberately thin. A fix that is correct and unroutable is not a fix,
so the implementation needs a timing check, not just the test below.

## The fix, as applied

`w_out_safe`'s ACT arm now also requires the rank-global windows:

```systemverilog
if (r_do_act) w_out_safe = bank_act_ready_i[RK0][r_bank]
                        && tfaw_ok_i[RK0] && trrd_ok_i[RK0];
```

The RTL had already written down the gap it was leaving. The mask comment read:

> tFAW/tRRD ... are re-checked later, at the STAGE-1b pre-pick
> (w_act_gate_live). NOT at the fire stage: this comment used to say "at the
> fire stage ... which is authoritative" ... w_out_safe re-validates an ACT
> against bank_act_ready_i only, which is PER-BANK, and tFAW/tRRD are
> rank-global. See pumice ISSUE-019.

So the defect was known, documented, and unfixed; what was missing was evidence
that it was reachable. Both comments are updated.

Dropping the ACT is lossless by the argument the block already makes for the
per-bank case: every CAM commit/issue is qualified by `w_fire_out`, so an
unfired entry stays schedulable and is re-picked. The windows are countdowns,
so a persistently closed gate cannot livelock.

The third row is the throughput check: re-picking a dropped ACT costs a cycle
or two, not a cliff.

### The formal row, added 2026-10-01

Three seeds is a measurement, not a proof, and the whole point of this bug is
that the violating alignment is rare. `formal/scoria/cmd_arbiter` composes the
arbiter with the REAL `scoria_global_timers` -- arbiter issues, timers observe,
timers gate the arbiter -- and times the ACTs with a counter the wrapper keeps
itself, so neither DUT is trusted to report its own spacing. With the CAM side
left entirely free (the strongest environment), no counterexample exists within
30 cycles of reset.

That is BOUNDED, and the bound should be quoted with the claim: it is "no
violation reachable in 30 cycles", not "no violation reachable ever". Depth 30
was chosen to span the property's own worst case -- four ACTs at tRRD >= 2 is
>= 8 cycles and the fifth must clear tFAW up to 18 -- so the tFAW window closes
inside the trace rather than being cut off by it. A k-induction proof would be
stronger and is not what this is.

The same proof is what found scoria BUG-002: the identical shape on the tZQCS
window, which simulation could not reproduce in 10 alignments.

## TIMING, MEASURED 2026-10-01 -- this gate is now ANSWERED

The claim above that "the repo has no out-of-context synthesis target for a
single module" was WRONG. It does: `rtl/amba/fpga/tcl/monitor_synth.tcl`, a
non-project OOC synth + place + route driven by environment variables, written
for the monitor characterization (amba TASK-034) and general enough to take any
module with a `*clk` port. `scoria_top` runs through it in ~3 minutes.

Measured on xc7k325tffg900-2 at 10 ns, with the two gates REVERTED and then
restored, same flow both times:

| | WNS reg-to-reg | fmax | LUTs | FFs | worst logic levels |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| without the gate(s) | -1.850 ns | 84.4 MHz | 11025 | 6827 | 31 |
| with the gate(s) | -2.022 ns | 83.2 MHz | 11025 | 6827 | 31 |

**Cost: 0.172 ns.** One AND level, which is what was added. Identical LUT, FF
and logic-level counts.

So the fix is NOT what breaks timing -- but scoria_top does not meet 100 MHz
either way, missing by 1.85 ns BEFORE any of this work. That is a separate and
larger problem, filed as **scoria BUG-003**, where the evidence and the
caveats live (no floorplan, and `dfi_clk` constrained at 10 ns rather than its
real 2.5 ns).

This gate is therefore answered in the only way it can be: the change does not
cause the timing failure, and reverting it would not fix it. What remains open
is BUG-003, not this.
