# BUG-003: scoria_top misses its 100 MHz design point by ~2 ns

Out-of-context synthesis, place and route of `scoria_top` on the Genesys 2 part
at the shipping design point (100 MHz / 10 ns MC clock) does not close:

| | WNS reg-to-reg | fmax (reg2reg) | LUTs | FFs | BRAM | worst logic levels |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| scoria_top, default implementation | **-2.022 ns** | **83.2 MHz** | 11025 | 6827 | 4 | 31 |
| scoria_top, high-effort implementation | **-1.262 ns** | **88.8 MHz** | 11133 | 6976 | 4 | 30 |

**Priority:** P1 for the board. Nothing in simulation is affected -- the
controller is functionally verified (221 tests, 9 formal blocks) -- but a build
at 100 MHz will not meet timing as the RTL stands, and 100 MHz is the HAS
Chapter 2.4 design point the whole timing table is derived from.
**Status:** OPEN. Measured 2026-10-01, the first time scoria has been
synthesised at all.

## It is NOT the BUG-001 / BUG-002 fixes

That was the question this run was set up to answer, and it is answered by
measurement rather than argument: the two gates were reverted and the identical
flow re-run.

| | WNS reg2reg | fmax | LUTs | FFs | logic levels |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| pre-fix (neither gate) | -1.850 ns | 84.4 MHz | 11025 | 6827 | 31 |
| with BUG-001 + BUG-002 gates | -2.022 ns | 83.2 MHz | 11025 | 6827 | 31 |

The fixes cost **0.172 ns** -- one AND level, consistent with what was added --
on a design that was already **1.85 ns short**. Identical LUT, FF and
logic-level counts both ways. So the timing gate on BUG-001 and BUG-002 is
answered: they are not what breaks timing, and reverting them would not fix it.

## Where the time goes

Both runs' worst path is the arbiter's pick cone, from a CAM pop register to a
pick-output register:

```
pre-fix:   u_core/u_sched/u_arbiter/r_wr_pop_reg[1][1]/C
        -> u_core/u_sched/u_arbiter/r_rd_col_q_reg[0]/D
           12 ns path: logic 1.755 ns (15%), route 10.067 ns (85%)

with fix:  u_core/u_sched/u_arbiter/r_wr_pop_reg[3][1]/C
        -> u_core/u_sched/u_arbiter/r_wr_act_q_reg[0]/D
           12 ns path: logic 1.922 ns (16%), route 10.103 ns (84%)
```

**85% route, 31 logic levels -- and BOTH matter.** An earlier version of this
file said "logic is only 1.9 ns of a 12 ns path, so there is little to win by
shortening the cone and a great deal to win by placing it". That was too quick.
The level breakdown is:

    Logic Levels: 31  (LUT3=2  LUT5=1  LUT6=26  MUXF7=2)

**26 LUT6 in series.** The depth is real, and depth and route are not
independent here: every level is another hop to route, so a cone this deep
cannot be placed well no matter where it sits.

### The cone crosses the arbiter/CAM boundary, repeatedly

Tracing the cells, the path does not stay in one block:

```
r_wr_pop                    (u_sched/u_arbiter)
  -> wr_act_f / wr_col_f masks, arg_sel, wr_col_s   (arbiter)
  -> wr_col_bank                                    (u_ifc/u_wr_cam)
  -> r_do_pre, w_guarded                            (arbiter again)
  -> r_wr_act_q                                     (arbiter)
  -> r_wr_act_q[7]_i_55                              (u_wr_cam again)
```

So one combinational path ping-pongs between the arbiter and the write CAM. That
is what makes it simultaneously 31 levels deep AND 10 ns of route: the two
macro blocks are placed apart and the signal crosses between them more than
once. This is the registered-feedback structure the arbiter's own prepick
comment describes ("a column or ACT/PRE now spends THREE cycles registered ...
this block has produced two silicon double-issue bugs from exactly this
registered-feedback latency").

## How much is strategy, and how much is structure -- measured

Same design, same part, same 10 ns, two implementation efforts:

| run | WNS reg2reg | fmax | LUTs | FFs | levels | wall |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| default (`opt_design`/`place_design`/`route_design`) | -2.022 ns | 83.2 MHz | 11025 | 6827 | 31 | 157 s |
| high effort | **-1.262 ns** | **88.8 MHz** | 11133 | 6976 | 30 | 539 s |

High effort = `synth_design -retiming`, `opt_design -directive
ExploreWithRemap`, `place_design -directive ExtraTimingOpt`, `route_design
-directive AggressiveExplore`, and a `phys_opt_design -directive
AggressiveExplore` after each of place and route.

**Strategy alone recovers 0.76 ns of the 2.02 ns**, for +108 LUT / +149 FF
(retiming adding registers). That leaves **1.26 ns** which strategy does not
reach, and the structure above says why.

## The two candidate levers, in the order the evidence supports

1. **Pblock the arbiter and the CAMs together.** The path crosses between them
   repeatedly, so co-locating them attacks the 10 ns route component directly.
   This is the `stream_char` monbus-CAM shape, where floorplanning was the fix.
   Cheapest to try and needs no RTL change.
2. **Break the cross-block cone.** 26 LUT6 in series through two blocks is the
   part floorplanning cannot remove. Registering the arbiter/CAM boundary is
   the obvious move and is also the dangerous one -- the prepick guard exists
   because adding registered latency there has twice produced double-issue
   bugs. Any attempt must be re-verified against the full suite AND
   `formal/scoria/cmd_arbiter`, which is exactly the gate that would catch a
   stale-image regression.

## What this measurement does NOT say

Stated because an OOC number is easy to over-read:

1. **No floorplan, default strategy.** OOC place-and-route has no placement
   guidance from a surrounding design and no Pblock. A real build can be better
   (directed placement, the arbiter kept local) or worse (congestion from the
   rest of the system). -2 ns OOC means the margin is not there BY DEFAULT; it
   does not mean a floorplanned build misses by 2 ns.
2. **`dfi_clk` was constrained at 10 ns, not its real 2.5 ns.** The flow applies
   one period to every clock port it finds, so the 400 MHz DFI domain is
   UNDER-constrained here and effectively untested. The failing path is in
   `aclk`, which IS correctly constrained at 10 ns -- but a real build must
   constrain `dfi_clk` at 2.5 ns and that domain could be worse.
3. Only `scoria_top` was synthesised, not a board top: no PHY, no clocking, no
   I/O. `build-scoria/` does not exist (see the FPGA gap below).

## Done when

A build meets timing at 100 MHz, or the design point is restated. Both are real
options and the choice is the owner's: pumice's recorded position is that "a
thin POSITIVE WNS is the design point", which argues for closing 100 MHz rather
than dropping to 83.

Floorplanning is the indicated first move, on the 85%-route evidence.

## How to reproduce

The repo DOES have an out-of-context synthesis flow -- BUG-001 and BUG-002 both
said it did not, which was wrong and is corrected there. It is
`rtl/amba/fpga/tcl/monitor_synth.tcl`, driven by environment:

```sh
export REPO_ROOT=$PWD BRIDGE_TOP=scoria_top BRIDGE_PART=xc7k325tffg900-2 \
       BRIDGE_CLK_NS=10.0 \
       FPGA_FILELIST=$PWD/projects/components/mem-ctrl-ip/scoria-ddr3-lpddr3/rtl/filelists/top/scoria_top.f \
       FPGA_PROJECT_ROOT=<somewhere writable>
cd $FPGA_PROJECT_ROOT && vivado -mode batch -source $REPO_ROOT/rtl/amba/fpga/tcl/monitor_synth.tcl
```

~3 minutes per run including place and route. It writes `summary.txt` with the
one-line numbers plus `timing_worst_reg2reg.txt`, which is the number that
belongs to the design -- the plain WNS also carries the OOC I/O convention
(inputs/outputs valid at 30% of the period), which on a module with scoria's
port count would otherwise dominate.
