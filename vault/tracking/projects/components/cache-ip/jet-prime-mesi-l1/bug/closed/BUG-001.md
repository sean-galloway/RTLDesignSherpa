# BUG-001: the pipelined pair fabric re-pointed a stalled memory AR at another slot, and the hold that fixes it let a slot free in its AR's handshake cycle

> Source: the fabric's formal safety harness (`formal/jet-prime/jet_prime_pair_fabric`,
> jet-prime TASK-001 step 4, 2026-11-17). `ap_mar_stable` failed at step 6 in
> the proof's first second; after the fix, `ap_free_complete` / `ap_mo` failed at
> step 15. No sim or board symptom: the pair rig (gate 21/21, FULL 155/155), the
> reference-rig gate and two bitstreams' 55-point sweeps had passed the step-3
> fabric.

**Priority:** P2 -- an AXI protocol violation on the memory port (A3.2.1), reachable
on the board whenever the memory's two-deep burst queue stalls the AR; functionally
masked because the slave samples at the handshake and the fabric's own state is
consistent at that cycle.
**Status:** CLOSED 2026-11-17 -- both fixed in `jet_prime_pair_fabric` the day they
were found; see the record at the end.
**Owner:** TBD
**Where:** `projects/components/cache-ip/jet-prime-mesi-l1/rtl/top/jet_prime_pair_fabric.sv`,
the memory AR issue (`w_mar_*`) and the slot release (`w_free_now`).

## What was wrong

1. The memory AR was the lowest-index slot ready to issue (AR taken, AC accepted,
   data class, not issued, no DataTransfer verdict), a combinational pick
   presented straight on `mem_arvalid` / `mem_arid` / `mem_araddr`. During an
   `arready` stall a lower slot's AC was accepted, it became the lowest
   candidate, and the presented AR changed ID and address with ARVALID high. A
   DataTransfer verdict landing on the presented slot withdrew ARVALID without a
   handshake -- the other half of the same violation.
2. With the AR held once presented (the fix for 1), a DataTransfer verdict can
   land on a held AR. The slot then becomes deliverable (its line replayed, the
   requester's retire seen) before its read has issued, and the release rule --
   "no read issued, or the read drained" -- fired in the very cycle the held AR
   handshook: `r_mem_issued` was still 0 at that edge, so the slot freed with a
   memory read outstanding under its ID. A later grant could reuse the slot
   while the stale beats were still to arrive.

Correct: a presented memory AR holds, unchanged, until the memory takes it; a
slot whose AR is presented this cycle is not free; a DataTransfer verdict on a
presented or issued AR drains the beats (it suppresses only an AR not yet
presented).

## Why nothing else saw it

The reference rig's `sdpram_slave_axi4_axi4` rarely has two bursts queued, so
`arready` rarely drops; when the re-pick did happen the slave took whichever
slot was presented at the handshake, and the fabric's state for that slot was
self-consistent, so the data was right. Nothing on the memory side of the pair
rig checked AR stability (the cache-side monlites do). Case 2 needs the
verdict to land on a held AR, which the step-3 fabric could not produce.

## Record

- Fix (2026-11-17, TASK-001 step 4): `r_mar_vld` / `r_mar_slot` hold the
  presented AR until the handshake; the CR's early-done rule excludes a slot
  whose AR is presented (not only one handshaking) this cycle; `w_free_now`
  excludes a slot whose AR is presented this cycle. Header and
  `docs/delta/02_pair_fabric.md` updated.
- Proof: `ap_mar_stable`, `ap_mar_legal` (held form), `ap_free_complete`,
  `ap_mo`; mutations m4 (no hold) and m5 (the old early-done rule) each fail
  the property written for them.
- DV: the pair-rig TB now checks a stalled memory AR holds (valid, id, addr)
  every cycle, and scores a memory AR after a DataTransfer verdict only when it
  was first presented after the verdict.
- Handbook: `vault/handbook/dv/formal.md`, "A combinational lowest-index pick
  behind a stallable ready is a stability bug waiting for the stall".
