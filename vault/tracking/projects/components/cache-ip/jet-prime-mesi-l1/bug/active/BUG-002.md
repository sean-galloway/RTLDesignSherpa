# BUG-002: a write-back victim passed on by a snoop still drained, and its stale bytes could land after the new owner's write-back

> Source: onyx ISSUE-002 (filed 2026-11-23 by design review during onyx
> TASK-001 D1, scoped then as an IO-port hazard). Reproduced 2026-11-24 in
> jet-prime's own pair rig between two caches, with no IO port, once a
> hot-set soak phase was added: 2 of 20 FULL cells failed in two minutes.

**Priority:** P1 -- silent memory corruption between two coherent caches:
memory ends holding a line older than the last write-back, and a later
refetch returns it. No error, no protocol violation at any port.
**Status:** ACTIVE 2026-11-24 -- fixed and simulation-verified; the formal
re-runs on the regenerated flats are in progress (record below).
**Owner:** TBD
**Where:** `rtl/fub/jet_prime_wb_buffer.sv` (the snoop effect),
`rtl/fub/jet_prime_control.sv` (the drain start, the snoop source),
`rtl/top/jet_prime_pair_fabric.sv` and onyx's `onyx_ccu.sv` (the write port).

## What was wrong

A dirty victim waits in the WB buffer until its drain's BRESP. A snoop that
matched it was answered from the buffer at Modified, and every snoop that
changes a Modified line passes the line on (DataTransfer + PassDirty, or
MakeInvalid). The buffer recorded the snoop's effect (`r_eff`) and nothing
ever read it: the victim still drained. jet's MAS ch02/03 states it as the
design: "an invalidated snarfed victim still drains -- the fabric already
took the data; the drain completes the memory update".

The drain writes the bytes the absorption already wrote. If the new owner
writes the line, evicts it, and its write-back lands first, the old drain
overwrites it. Seed 83331, `s016w2l64b64_m02v1_lru_mon_full`, line 0x1b9:

| cycle | event |
|---|---|
| 75021 | cache 1 evicts the line (M), captures D0 |
| 75023 | the fabric grants cache 0's ReadUnique of the line |
| 75031 | cache 1's drain starts; the fabric holds its AW (the slot has no CR) |
| 75041 | the snoop hits cache 1's WB entry: DT + PD with D0, absorbed |
| 75062 | cache 0's fill completes with D0; it writes beat 7 (D1) |
| 75071 / 75081 | cache 0 evicts the line, its drain starts |
| 75104 | cache 0's write-back of D1 lands (it won the round-robin) |
| 75117 | cache 1's held drain of D0 lands over it |

A second form, not reached in sim but open in the RTL: the entry stayed in
the snarf CAM after passing its line on, so a second snoop before the BRESP
(the IO's, or a third cache's) was answered again from the stale bytes with
PassDirty.

Correct (ACE's rule is the master's): a line passed on dirty is not written
back, and no further snoop is answered from it.

## The fix (2026-11-24)

1. `jet_prime_wb_buffer`: a snoop effect on an entry whose drain has not
   started ends it -- out of the CAM, retired at the head without a drain
   (`r_dead`). An entry whose drain has started (`drain_start_i` seen, no
   BRESP) cannot withdraw its AW: it drains, and is marked passed
   (`sn_passed_o`) -- still in the CAM for the own-lookup drain-race stall,
   no longer a snoop source.
2. `jet_prime_control`: `drain_start` is held while a WB-sourced snoop on
   the head entry (`sn_head_o`) is granted or in service, so the head's drain
   cannot start between the grant and the effect; `w_src_wb` excludes a
   passed entry. (The first version, 9a1617aa5, held every drain start during
   any WB-sourced snoop; the control liveness proof failed `ap_drain_progress`
   at step 62, its 24-cycle bound -- a snoop granted in its entry's BRESP
   cycle retires that entry, and the next head's drain waited out the rest
   of the service. Narrowed to the head the same day.)
3. `jet_prime_pair_fabric` and `onyx_ccu`: two write-backs to one line are
   forwarded in presentation order (`r_aw_pres` / `r_aw_yield`): the drain of
   part 1's exception is the older AW, and the new owner's must wait for it.

Write-up: [delta 05](../../../../../../../../projects/components/cache-ip/jet-prime-mesi-l1/docs/delta/05_wb_ownership.md).

## Why the suites missed it

Five layers each had a reason not to see it, and the first is the root:

1. **The rule was the spec.** jet's MAS wrote the drain as intended
   behaviour, and the effect record that was meant to "make the drain write
   back the correct thing" had no reader. A write-only register is a design
   intent nobody finished; nothing in any flow flags state that is written
   and never read.
2. **The unit test asserted the rule.** `_scen_snarf_invalidated_still_drains`
   in `jet_prime_wb_buffer_tb.py` checked that a snarfed victim drains. A
   test that encodes the defect locks it in.
3. **The core rig has no second writer.** `jet_prime_core_tb.py` targets 40 %
   of its snoops at WB lines, but its fabric is the testbench: one cache, and
   memory written only by that cache's drains. A duplicate drain writes the
   same bytes, so it is indistinguishable from a correct one there.
4. **The pair rig's model restated the RTL's rule.** `jet_prime_pair_rig_tb.py`
   answered every WB-hit snoop at M and kept the entry until its BRESP, and
   wrote the drain into model memory at the drain start. A stale second answer
   would have been scored as correct. Only the data comparison could catch the
   drain, and only when the fabric's write order diverged from the model's.
5. **The stimulus never collided.** The uniform soak spreads two CPUs over
   SPAN lines (at least 16 x SETS), so a full cell reached a WB snarf 0-6
   times, one of them forced by the ships-in-the-night scenario, which checks
   only that memory holds the dirty line after quiescence (true either way).
   The hot-set phase (both CPUs, 70 % writes, `WAYS + 3` tags in two sets)
   raised that to 21-102 snarfs per cache per cell and failed two cells in its
   first run.

And the review that found it under-scoped it: onyx ISSUE-002 named the
mechanism but filed it as an IO-port hazard, and onyx gained an IO-only
ordering rule. The same hole was open between two caches on both managers.

The first fix's own defect (the hold on every drain start, found by the
control liveness proof) was invisible to simulation for a simpler reason:
no TB checked how long an eligible drain waits for its start. A hold added
by a fix is a new wait, and a new wait needs a latency check in the same
change. `jet_prime_control_tb.py` now has one (`_drain_progress_watch`:
from the TB's own records -- the head is the expected capture FIFO's front,
the snooped line is the one the TB presented -- a drain may wait at most 2
cycles unless a snoop is on its own line) and a scenario that sets the case
up (`drain_not_held_off_head`: a snarf granted in the head's BRESP cycle,
a second victim behind it). Restoring the first fix's hold fails it in all
three passes (6 mismatches, the next head held 10 cycles).

The formal harnesses could not have caught it: the control proof drove the
effect but stated nothing about the drain after it, and both fabric proofs
take the caches' AWs as free inputs with no cross-port ordering property.

## Verification

- WB buffer unit: new scenarios (the entry ends under both effects; a
  passed-on entry behind the head is skipped; a draining head is passed and
  still drains); gate / func / full PASS.
- Control unit: the BRESP-race scenario reworked; a second snoop to a passed
  entry answers the Invalid row with no effect.
- Pair rig: the hot-set phase plus a write-back order check at the cache
  ports (`_check_wb_order`, independent of the fabric). jet-prime full
  155/155 from clean; every full cell ends 5-31 entries and drains 16-75
  passed ones per cache.
- Drain progress (the liveness find, in sim): the control TB's watcher and
  `drain_not_held_off_head`; control gate / func / full PASS from clean; M3,
  the first fix's hold restored, fails all three passes.
- Mutations: M1 (the fabric's order hold removed) fails seed 116 of 24, the
  order check first, then the data; M2 (entries never ended) fails 26 of 26
  pair-rig cells.
- Formal: control `ap_wb_no_drain_in_svc`, `ap_wb_ended_never_drains`, covers
  `cp_wb_ended` (step 42) and `cp_wb_passed_snoop` (step 44); pair fabric and
  onyx `ap_wb_order`, cover `cp_wb_order_wait` (step 4). Covers reached;
  proofs running (record below).
- onyx: its pair rig model and soak carry the same rule; the soak's IO now
  issues ReadOnce / WriteUnique / WriteLineUnique to the hot lines.
