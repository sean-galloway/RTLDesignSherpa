# BUG-002: close-page modes reach only 62.8% of their own command-bus ceiling

**Status:** CLOSED 2026-09-25 (fixed: 62.8% -> 85.7% of ceiling)  **Priority:** P2 — bounds what close-page paging
can ever be worth on silicon; the board ships open page, so not a shipping
regression

Re-filed as a BUG on Sean's call 2026-09-24. It was tracked as [[ISSUE-002]]
and closed there with the residual ACCEPTED, on the grounds that the remaining
cost is pick-pipeline and bank-timer flop stages which Sean had ruled by design
for [[ISSUE-001]]. Sean's position now: a measured shortfall against a computed
ceiling is a DEFECT, whatever its cause, and belongs in the bug lane where it
will be seen. ISSUE-001 itself is dropped.

Per the convention an issue that turns out to be a defect is closed as an issue
and re-filed as a bug with each naming the other, so the trail is
[[ISSUE-002]] (closed, carries the full investigation) -> this.

## Expected vs observed

The arbiter issues at most ONE DFI command per cycle, so no mode can exceed
`BL_WORDS / commands_per_access` beats per cycle. Board geometry, 8-bank
rotation, refresh parked:

| mode | cmds/access | expected (ceiling) | observed | of ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| build_default / fixed_open | 1.04 | 96.0% | 98.97% | at it |
| static_open / adapt_time | 1.08 | 92.3% | 95.05% | at it |
| adapt_access | 1.09 | 91.9% | 92.75% | at it |
| **static_close / rbl_static** | **2.04** | **49.0%** | **30.77%** | **62.8%** |
| **rbl_dyn** | **1.64** | **61.0%** | **41.56%** | **68.1%** |

Every open-page mode sits at its ceiling. The close-page family does not.

## Root cause, established

From the DFI command trace: `ACT@130 ACT@136 ACT@140 ... WR@170` -- the first
column lands **8 aclk after its ACT** while tRCD is 3. The ~5-cycle excess is
the pick pipeline plus bank-timer registration on the ACT->column path, and
under CLOSE page every access pays it. During that window both candidate
classes are empty, so there is nothing for the pipeline to carry.

Ruled out by measurement, do not re-try: tRRD (30.77% at 1, 2 AND 4), tRCD/tRP/
tRC (37.5% with all three at minimum), tFAW, CAM depth (8 -> 16 gives
30.77 -> 32.43), the bank guards, bank recovery, and the outstanding dial
(board: static_close flat at 33.9 MB/s across OS 8/16/32 -- not latency-bound,
not outstanding-bound).

One partial fix already landed: removing the classify-time ACT mask's global
tFAW/tRRD gate took rbl_dyn 32.32% -> 41.56% (+29%). static_close did not move,
because its stall window has no columns available to carry.

## What fixing it would require

Shortening the ACT->column path -- i.e. removing a pick-pipeline stage or the
bank-timer registration stage. That is the trade this design makes on purpose
(flop stages for timing closure on a part at +0.016 ns WNS), which is why it
was accepted before. Filing it as a bug does not change the engineering; it
changes where the cost is recorded, so it is not mistaken for "characterized
and fine".

## Guard in place

`perf_paging_sweep` asserts a floor at 0.55 x ceiling and REPORTS the ratio
either way, so a step change below the measured point fails. Mutation-proven
2026-09-24: tightening the floor to 0.95 fails with the per-mode gap in the
message. That guard stays whatever happens to this bug -- it catches regression,
not the accepted shortfall.

**Do not re-open the 63% chase without a new idea.** The measured dead ends are
listed above; the only untried lever is pipeline depth, which is the design
point.


## 2026-09-24 — FIX ATTEMPTED: speculative column behind its ACT. Made it WORSE.

The obvious fix short of removing a pipeline stage, tried and measured. It does
not work, and the reason is architectural rather than a detail -- so this rules
out the whole approach rather than one implementation of it.

**The idea.** A column is blocked from even ENTERING the pipeline while its
bank's ACT is in flight, so under close page it only starts its own 3-4 stage
journey after the ACT fires and tRCD expires. Instead: treat the bank/row an
in-flight ACT is about to open as a PROSPECTIVE hit, let the column ride down
the pipeline behind its own ACT, and re-check the bank LIVE at the fire stage.
Exactly the shape of the fix that removed the classify-time tFAW/tRRD gate and
kept `w_act_gate_live` (+29% on rbl_dyn): classify optimistically, fire
authoritatively.

**Implemented** as `w_pend_act` / `w_pend_row` (from `rd_act_f`/`wr_act_f` plus
the selection-cycle `w_sel_*_act_f`), a prospective arm on `rhit`/`whit`, the
same arm on the `r_bank_rdwr_ready` term, a narrow relaxation of
`w_preact_bank_guard` for ONLY the column whose row that ACT opens, a live
`bank_rdwr_ready_i` gate on both column fire branches, and the same live gate
qualifying `w_c_col` so an unfirable column could not win the class vote.
Lints clean.

**Measured (board geometry, same sweep):**

| mode | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| static_close / rbl_static | 30.77% | **28.57%** |
| rbl_dyn | 41.56% | 41.29% |

**Worse, not better.** Reverted; baseline reproduces at 30.77 / 41.56 exactly.

**Why, and this is the part worth keeping.** The pre-pick stage is IN-ORDER: a
column that has been selected occupies the slot until it fires. Qualifying
`w_c_col` with the live bank gate stopped an unfirable column winning the CLASS
VOTE, but not consuming the SLOT -- so the speculative column sits in the
pipeline waiting for tRCD while blocking the ACT behind it. The optimism costs
more than the serialisation it removes.

**So the ACT->column serialisation is not a conservative gate that can be
relaxed. It is a consequence of an in-order pick pipeline**, and removing it
needs either out-of-order issue at the pre-pick stage (a rearchitecture) or
fewer stages (PUMICE-017/018 added them deliberately for timing, and this block
has already produced two silicon double-issue bugs from registered-feedback
latency).

**Do not retry the speculative-column approach.** Any variant hits the same
in-order slot occupancy. The remaining levers are the two above, and both are
large.


## 2026-09-25 — WHY the guards exist, and why the obvious cure is unsound

Sean: *"Why are things explicitly blocked. That is anathema to my intent. The
bank timers should indicate safe. Once safe anything can proceed."*

The intent is right and `bank_timer.sv` was built to it -- its own header says
`safe_*` is a combinational AND of the counters, "a single register stage …
with no multi-stage FSM lag (which was the root of the refresh-vs-ACT /
column-vs-PRE hazards)".

**The gap: the timers are fed at the LAST stage.** `evt_act_o = w_fire_out &&
r_do_act` -- the `set_*` strobes are driven at FIRE, while commands are
SELECTED 3-4 registered stages earlier. So up to four commands are in flight
that the timers have never been told about, and `safe_*` says safe because it
has not seen them. Every guard in the arbiter exists to cover that one gap:

    w_guarded  w_prepick_guard  w_preact_bank_guard  w_col_inflight_guard
    w_ap_col_guard  w_pre_col_guard  w_inflight_col  w_inflight_preact
    w_ref_col_block

Nine masks answering the question the timers should answer. They are not a
second safety mechanism; they are compensation for feeding the timers late.

**The design already contains the right pattern, applied once.** `r_tccd_fwd`
loads on `w_col_sel_now` -- SELECTION, not fire -- with explicit pipeline
accounting ("`<= 1`, not `== 0`: a column classified while the counter reads 1
is SELECTED the next cycle, exactly tCCD after the previous selection"). tCCD
therefore needs no guard. Every constraint that is not forward-looking needed
one.

**So: drive the bank timers at selection and delete the guards?** Checked, and
NO -- for two independent reasons, either of which is fatal.

1. **Selection is not a commitment.** The pre-pick registers load under
   `else if (w_out_ready)`, i.e. they are OVERWRITTEN with a fresh `w_sel_*`
   every ready cycle. The RTL says so: *"the pre-pick is a re-evaluated
   decision, not a stream that must not lose a beat"*. A command selected this
   cycle can be superseded next cycle, so a selection-time `set_*` would be
   for a command that never happens.
2. **The bank timer carries ROW STATE, not just counters.** `set_act_i` sets
   `r_row_valid <= 1` and `r_open_row <= row_i`. A spurious ACT therefore marks
   a row OPEN that was never opened -- and columns are classified against
   exactly that state. That is not a conservative error that costs throughput;
   it issues columns to a bank that is not open. Data corruption.

This is also why `r_tccd_fwd` gets away with it: tCCD is pure spacing, so an
early load only delays the next column. Loading a bank timer early cannot be
made conservative, because the row state it carries is load-bearing for
correctness.

**What that leaves.** Making the timers authoritative requires selection to
become a commitment (a non-re-evaluated pick -- a different arbiter), or a
separate speculative row-state shadow that tracks in-flight ACTs and is
reconciled on fire -- which is the guards again, just centralised. The second
is arguably worth doing for clarity: ONE structure answering "what is in
flight" instead of nine ad-hoc masks, with the same semantics. It would not
change performance, and it would make the ACT->column serialisation explicit
instead of emergent.

**It does not fix this bug.** The serialisation is the in-order pre-pick
pipeline (see the failed speculative-column attempt above), not the guards.


## 2026-09-25 — FIXED to 85.7% of ceiling. Closing.

The "new idea" this bug was waiting for turned out to be Sean's: make the bank
timers say when a bank WILL be safe, and move enforcement to the last stage.

**What landed** (`c1c02c3e7`, RTL; `0bb17efff`, DV):

1. **Final-stage timing authority.** The output register is re-checked against
   the LIVE `bank_*_ready_i` and cannot fire until it says safe. A rejected
   pick is DROPPED, not held -- holding freezes `w_out_ready` and head-of-line
   blocks the pipeline, which is exactly the 30.77 -> 28.57 regression recorded
   above. Dropping is lossless: every CAM commit is qualified by `w_fire_out`,
   so an unfired entry stays schedulable and is re-picked. Behaviourally a
   no-op on its own (30.77 / 41.56 unchanged) -- it exists to make the next
   part safe.
2. **Advisory lookahead.** `bank_timer` exports `safe_{act,rdwr,pre}_la_o`:
   `r_X <= LA` is exactly "reaches 0 within LA cycles" for a saturating
   down-counter. The pick pipeline decides on these; the final stage enforces
   the live set. `BANK_LA=4` = the select-to-fire stage count.

**Result, board geometry:**

| mode | before | after | of ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|
| static_close / rbl_static | 30.77% | **42.01%** | 62.8% -> **85.7%** |
| rbl_dyn | 41.56% | **53.04%** | 68.1% -> **87.0%** |

**Confirmed on silicon** by an isolated A/B -- same bitstream, only `BANK_LA`
0 -> 4: close-page reads +32.8% (33.9 -> 45.0 MB/s of a 600 MB/s port),
page-hostile col_major +19.2%, streaming +0.5%. Sim predicted +36.5%.
WNS +0.073 ns, so it cost no timing.

The gain lands where the mechanism says it should: large where bank timers
bind, negligible where the port already binds.

### Two dead ends, both measured, both now closed

**AP-predictive lookahead: no effect.** An auto-precharging bank reads unsafe
for ACT though its close is autonomous, so the arm was added --
ETA = `max(preblk,ras) + 1 + t_rp` (NOT `r_rp <= LA`: r_rp has not been loaded
yet and reads the previous precharge's residue, which is optimistic in the one
direction that matters). Measured ZERO change at LA 4/6/8/10/12, and forcing
`safe_act_la_o = 1'b1` outright also changed nothing. REVERTED, not shipped.

Why it cannot work: the AP ETA is 5-7 cycles while select-to-fire is 3-4. The
arm predicts a bank ready LATER than the command arrives, so the pick is
selected, reaches the final gate still unsafe, and is dropped. This is also the
real reason the LA sweep saturates at 3: **lookahead beyond the select-to-fire
distance is unactionable by construction.** LA 8 and 12 measure identical to 4.

**tFAW/tRRD: still not the limiter.** The new stall counters read
`actlimit=126 (70.4%)` on static_close, which looked like the answer. It is
not -- relaxing tFAW/tRRD from 6/2 to 1/1 leaves throughput AND the counter
bit-identical (42.01%, 126). Tightening to 15/15 does bite (16.57%, 630), so
the counter is real; there is simply slack at 6/2.

**The instrument misleads here, and that is worth recording.** The 7-way
classifier is PRIORITY-ORDERED and has no category for pipeline serialisation.
Cycles where the in-order pre-pick has nothing to offer fall through to
whichever timing term happens to be unsatisfied -- here actlimit. A high share
proves that term was UNSATISFIED, never that relieving it would help. Always
confirm a stall verdict by moving the knob. The earlier "tRRD ruled out by
measurement" note above was right.

### Residual, and why this closes

14.3% of ceiling remains, and it is the in-order pick pipeline documented
above -- the trade this design makes deliberately for timing closure on a part
at +0.073 ns WNS. Removing it needs out-of-order issue at the pre-pick stage
or fewer stages, both rearchitectures, both previously ruled the design point.

Closing at 85.7% of ceiling with the cause understood and the 0.55 floor guard
still in place. **Do not retry:** speculative columns (above), AP-predictive
lookahead, LA > select-to-fire depth, or tFAW/tRRD relaxation.
