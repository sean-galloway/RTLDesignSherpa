# Peer-Paper Reference Archive for `rtl/amba`

Seminal peer-reviewed (and, where noted, canonical non-peer) references for every
design technique implemented across the `rtl/amba` SystemVerilog library
(240 modules). Most of the area implements published protocol specifications and textbook techniques, so the primary citation for a protocol module is the Arm specification section that defines its behaviour. The ocelus subsystem is an original design; its entries list related prior art, not sources. The archive exists so that anyone reading or extending the RTL can
go straight to the original source of each technique rather than a textbook
retelling. The companion archive for the arithmetic library is
[`rtl/math/references/README.md`](../../math/references/README.md).

## Acquisition Policy

Only legitimately open-access copies are archived:

- Author or lab homepages (author-hosted copies of IEEE/ACM papers)
- Standards bodies and open specification releases (Wishbone B4, IEEE-ISTO 5001)
- Author-published practitioner papers (SNUG papers released by their authors), retrieved
  from the Internet Archive where the original host no longer serves them
- Patent offices (Google Patents copies of U.S. patents)
- Institutional repositories, university technical reports, and preservation archives
  (Internet Archive)

Every downloaded file was opened and its first page verified to be the cited
document; HTML error pages and mislabeled files were rejected. Papers that exist
only behind a paywall are listed as **citation only** with a DOI link. Textbook
treatments are cited by book, author and chapter only and are never archived.
Vendor application notes are deliberately not used as references.

Protocol specifications are handled separately:

- **Arm AMBA specifications** (IHI 0022 AXI/ACE, IHI 0024 APB, IHI 0051 AXI-Stream)
  are cited by document ID, issue and section, with a developer.arm.com link, and are
  **not archived** because their redistribution terms are unclear. AXI3/AXI4,
  AXI4-Lite and ACE behaviour is cited from IHI 0022 **Issue H** (the last issue that
  defines them; Issue J removed them and renumbered every chapter). AXI5 behaviour is
  cited from **Issue L**. A single citation never mixes section numbers from two issues.
- **Openly released specifications** whose own terms permit redistribution are archived:
  Wishbone B4 (OpenCores) and IEEE-ISTO 5001 Nexus.
- Papers already archived for `rtl/cdc` or `rtl/common` are linked there, not duplicated.

File naming: `<Technique>__<FirstAuthor><Year>.pdf`.

## Technique → Module(s) → Reference Index

### GAXI elastic buffers and FIFOs

The `gaxi_*` blocks are the generic valid/ready infrastructure every AMBA protocol
module in `rtl/amba` is built on. The handshake itself is defined by the Arm
specifications; the buffering theory behind the skid buffer and register slice is
latency-insensitive / elastic design. The FIFO full/empty mechanics are the
`rtl/common` and `rtl/cdc` techniques and are cited from those archives, not
duplicated here.

characterization stimulus/check blocks (LFSR pattern sources and CRC-32 sinks),
bus-utilization and latency instruments, an AMBA-side clock-gate wrapper, and the
simple-dual-port RAM slaves. Most of it is protocol mechanics defined by the AXI
specification; the integrity and power techniques reuse the `rtl/common` archive.

### AXI4 and AXI5 protocol specification

Every module in `rtl/amba/axi4` and `rtl/amba/axi5` is defined first by the
AXI specification, ARM IHI 0022, and the rows below give the issue and section
for each behavior a module relies on. Two issues are cited. **Issue H**
(*AMBA AXI and ACE Protocol Specification*, 31 Mar. 2020,
[developer.arm.com/documentation/ihi0022/h](https://developer.arm.com/documentation/ihi0022/h/))
is the last issue that defines the AXI3 and AXI4 interfaces; Issue J (Mar. 2023)
removed AXI3, AXI4, AXI4-Lite, ACE and ACE5 and renumbered every chapter.
**Issue L** (*AMBA AXI Protocol Specification*, 27 Aug. 2025,
[developer.arm.com/documentation/ihi0022/latest](https://developer.arm.com/documentation/ihi0022/latest/))
is the current issue for AXI5 features. Arm specifications are cited only and
never archived, because their redistribution terms are unclear.

### APB (APB4, APB5, APBX crossbars, shims)

The APB modules are protocol endpoints: a master or slave state machine that converts between the APB SETUP/ACCESS sequence and a valid/ready cmd/rsp pair buffered through `gaxi_skid_buffer`. The protocol itself has no peer-reviewed origin; the Arm specification is the defining document and is cited, not archived (Arm's redistribution terms are unclear). The techniques underneath (skid buffers, async FIFOs, idle-timeout clock gating, parity) are cited to their papers, most of them already archived in `rtl/cdc` or `rtl/common`.

## Notes

- **AXI specification issue.** Issue H (ARM IHI 0022H, 2020) is cited because it is the last issue that still contains AXI3/AXI4/AXI4-Lite alongside AXI5; Issue J (2023) removed the AXI4 content and renumbered the chapters (handshake is A3.2 "Channel handshake" there). Section numbers above were checked against the Issue H text. Arm specifications are citation only. The `developer.arm.com/documentation/<doc>/<issue>` URLs follow Arm's versioned-path scheme; the site refuses scripted access, so the landing pages could not be fetched and checked.
- **Skid buffer vs register slice.** `gaxi_skid_buffer` registers both `rd_valid` and `wr_ready`, i.e. Lf = Lb = 1 in Cortadella's terms; Property 3.1 then forces capacity >= 2, which is exactly the module's `DEPTH >= 2` guard. `gaxi_regslice` is the one-entry Lf = 1, Lb = 0 stage, so its ready path is combinational from `rd_ready`; it cuts only the forward timing path.
- **BILBO author order.** Crossref lists the JSSC paper as Könemann, Zwiehoff, Mucha; Semantic Scholar and the usual citation give Könemann, Mucha, Zwiehoff (the order of the 1979 ITC "Built-In Logic Block Observation Techniques" paper). The latter is used. The BILBO lineage is cited for the generator-plus-compactor architecture; the RTL compares returned data beat-by-beat and accumulates a CRC rather than a MISR.
- **Peer-agent duplicate avoided.** The Cortadella DAC 2006 paper is archived once as `ElasticBuffer__Cortadella2006.pdf` and shared by the gaxi and AXI4-Lite rows.
- **Which issue to cite.** Section numbers for AXI4 behavior are from Issue H, the last issue that describes AXI4. Issue E (2013) uses the same chapter A3 layout but was not re-checked section by section. Issue J and later renumber everything. Chapter A3 "Single Interface Requirements" is split there into A2 "AXI transport" and A3 "AXI transactions", and the AMBA 5 chapters E1/E2 are folded into Part A. Do not mix Issue H and Issue L section numbers in one cite.
- **`axi5_atomic_filter` and the R-beat rule.** IHI 0022H §E1.1.8 (Issue L §A6.4.8) requires that for AtomicLoad, AtomicSwap and AtomicCompare "a Subordinate must send the correct number of read data transfers, even if the write response is DECERR or SLVERR". The filter originally returned only a local DECERR on B; since #102 it also returns the required DECERR R beats (AWLEN+1, or (AWLEN>>1)+1 for AtomicCompare) with RID = AWID, merged with downstream R traffic.
- **The atomic ID rule.** Issue H states only that an atomic must not share an ID with an outstanding non-atomic transaction, nor with another outstanding atomic. Issue L §A6.4.4 restates this as "unique-in-flight", with separate rules for AtomicStore (write channels) and the read-return classes (read and write channels). `axi5_atomic_rr_tracker` keys on ID alone and relies on exactly this rule.
- **AXI5 signal names corrected under #102.** The wrappers previously carried non-spec MTE and unique-ID ports. They now match Issue L:
  - `rtagmatch`, `btag` and `awtag` were removed. Neither Issue H nor Issue L defines them: the spec reports a tag match only on the write response (BTAGMATCH, with BCOMP), returns tags on reads as RTAG, and carries write tags on WTAG.
  - `btagmatch` is now 2 bits, matching H §E1.15 Table E1-26 (encodings 0b00-0b11). BCOMP is not carried.
  - `awunique`/`arunique` became `awidunq`/`aridunq`, and `bidunq`/`ridunq` were added (H §E1.13, L §A5.2). AWUNIQUE is a different, ACE-family signal tied to WriteEvict.
- **Wake-up (#102).** The spec defines AWAKEUP so that an interface can be clock-gated without losing the first cycle of a request. The wrappers now carry `awakeup` behind `ENABLE_WAKEUP`, driven as the upstream AWAKEUP OR the registered request valids, and the `_cg` wrappers add it to their wake term so a request arriving after idle no longer waits for the clock to restart.
- **Subtractive decode** has no peer-reviewed origin. The AXI specification's own default-slave recommendation (H §A3.4.5) is the defining document and is cited instead.
- `axi4_dwidth_converter.md` previously cited "IHI 0022E, Chapter 11: Data Width Conversion". IHI 0022 has no such chapter, so the cite was corrected to the narrow-transfer and byte-lane sections (H §A3.4.4 "Data read and write structure"; L §A3.2.3 "Narrow transfers").
- `axi4/README.md` previously cited "ARM IHI 0022D ... (AXI3/AXI4/AXI5)". Issue D (Oct. 2011) predates AXI5 (first defined in Issue F, Dec. 2017), so that cite was replaced with Issues H and L.
- AXI5-Lite signal presence (Issue L Table B2.2, AXI5-Lite column): AWLOCK/ARLOCK, AxMPAM, AxMECID, AxNSAID and AxLOOP are **N (not present)**, and §B2.1.5 rules out exclusive access. The `axil5_*` modules carried all five behind `ENABLE_*` options defaulting to 1; #102 removed them.
- AxID on AXI5-Lite is **YS** in Table B2.2 (optional on a Manager, mandatory on a Subordinate). Since #102 the `axil5_*` modules carry AWID/BID and ARID/RID with an `ID_WIDTH` parameter (0 allowed on the manager side, 1..32 on the subordinate side). The `_mon`/`_monlite` wrappers still attribute responses to ID 0, which is recorded as #102 item 8.
- AXI4-Lite data width must be 32 or 64 bits (IHI 0022H §B1.1.2). Since #102 every `axil4_*` module enforces it with an elaboration-time parameter guard; `axil5_*` modules accept the AXI5 widths 8..1024 (powers of two).
- Arm specifications are cited, not archived (redistribution terms unclear). Issue H was used for AXI4-Lite because Issues J, K and L no longer contain it.
- The Cortadella 2006 PDF is the authors' UPCommons self-archive (handle 2117/133566), found through OpenAlex; its first page was checked against the cited title and authors.
- **APB5 and AXI5-Stream parity (#102).** IHI 0024E §5.3 and IHI 0051B ch. 5 require *odd* parity on signals named `*CHK`, one check bit per 8 bits of the protected signal. The modules previously computed even parity on `*PARITY` ports and folded `PSTRB` into the control check. They now carry the spec's `*CHK` signals (APB5: `PADDRCHK`, `PCTRLCHK`, `PSELCHK`, `PENABLECHK`, `PWDATACHK`, `PSTRBCHK`, `PRDATACHK`, `PREADYCHK`, `PSLVERRCHK`, `PWAKEUPCHK` and the user-signal checks; AXI5-Stream: `TDATACHK`, `TSTRBCHK`, `TLASTCHK`, `TIDCHK`, `TDESTCHK`, `TUSERCHK`, `TVALIDCHK`, `TREADYCHK`, `TWAKEUPCHK`), computed with `rtl/common/dataint_parity` per 8-bit piece. #102 also corrected APB5 `PWAKEUP` to a requester (master) output (IHI 0024E §3.7.2, Table 3-7).
- **ACE scope (#102).** The ACE ports carry `ARSNOOP`/`AWSNOOP`, `RACK`/`WACK` and the AC/CR/CD channels. Before #102, `ARDOMAIN`/`AWDOMAIN` and `ARBAR`/`AWBAR` were not on the ports and `RRESP` was 2 bits. They are now carried (IHI 0022H.c Tables D2-1 and D2-2), `RRESP` is 4 bits with `RRESP[3:2]` = IsShared, PassDirty (Table D2-3), and the slave movers forward `RACK`/`WACK` (§D2.3). `AWUNIQUE` is not carried: it is required only by components that support WriteEvict.
- **Spec issue numbering.** IHI 0024 Issue D numbers wake-up and user signaling §3.6/§3.7. Issue E inserts §3.6 RME and shifts them to §3.7/§3.8. IHI 0022 Issues J and later drop ACE (Arm now marks ACE as superseded by CHI), so Issue H.c is the last issue to define it.
- **Shared PDFs.** Papamarcos & Patel 1984 and the Nagarajan et al. Primer (2nd ed.) were already archived in `projects/components/cache-ip/References/` and are linked rather than duplicated. The existing Primer file name there credits "Sorin_Hill_Wood"; the 2nd edition's first author is V. Nagarajan, and the citation here uses the correct author list. Carloni 2001 and Cortadella 2006 are shared with the GAXI rows.
- The Sweazey & Smith copy carries one reference-list entry naming a processor vendor; it is a bibliography line, not an affiliation.
- **Framing.** The ocelus packet format, the lite ocelus's linked-list tracker, the two-drain group and the template compressor were designed in this repository. Every peer entry above is phrased as related work or comparison. None is cited as an origin, and the ocelus bus is not compatible with any of the cited standards.
- **Nexus.** IEEE-ISTO 5001 is the only standard in the table. It is cited because it defines the vocabulary the ocelus's packet fields line up with: message type, source client, overrun reporting and timestamps. The archived copy is the 2003 Version 2.0 PDF that the Nexus 5001 Forum published on its site (retrieved from the Internet Archive). Its copyright notice allows copying and distribution of the unmodified document. Later revisions of the standard exist and were not used.
- **Move-to-front versus true LRU.** `monbus_cam` keeps the most recently hit template at slot 0 and ages the others down. That is the move-to-front list of Bentley et al. 1986 kept in a CAM, and the template index works as a move-to-front rank. The compressor was tuned on the repo's own trace data; Uzelac & Milenković is a related hardware use of move-to-front for program traces, not a model the RTL follows.
- **Per-template delta timestamp.** Coding each template's timestamp against that template's previous one is comparable to the Nexus "address thread" (an address coded relative to the last address of the same kind, §5.2). It was reached independently.
